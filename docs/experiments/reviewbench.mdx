title: "ReviewBench: the treatment did not land"
description: "The proposed review-efficiency benefit was not demonstrated."
---

Localization suggested that reusable structure might reduce context gathering. ReviewBench tested that implication where the reviewer already has a diff and must support valid defects.

The comparison was plain source access versus the same access with optional structural packets. The pilot had ten paired tasks and mixed preserved protocol versions. The heldout comparison had 24 tasks, 48 observations, and 224 positive references.

| Corrected holdout view | Plain valid / candidates | Packet valid / candidates | Plain recall | Packet recall |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Final delivery | 5/9 | 4/6 | 10.3% | 7.1% |
| Final + recoverable checkpoints | 18/32 | 17/27 | 36.2% | 27.7% |

Inclusive precision was 56.3% versus 63.0%. Checkpoint recovery is not final delivery.

## Why the negative result matters

All three packet calls across pilot and holdout were refused. No accepted packet established treatment uptake. Therefore the study cannot claim packet-induced token savings, a review speedup, or validation of the proposed 5×/10× efficiency targets.

A within-arm matching correction reinstated four packet-arm findings. A later denominator repair retained every heldout task and all 224 positives in each micro-recall view, including failed or empty tasks. Earlier values remain historical.

Holdout review used 6,567,947 provider-total tokens, with 276,637 evaluator tokens recorded separately. Actual plan-billed cost is unknown. The guard stopped 23/48 holdout slots. The `obs-3` usage discrepancy remains unresolved.

The lesson is operational: an available tool is not an adopted treatment. A localization improvement does not automatically translate into a review-efficiency gain. [Evidence and replay](/experiments/evidence) preserve corrected tables and unavailable timings.

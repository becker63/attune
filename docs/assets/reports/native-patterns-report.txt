# Native Datalog, localization, and refactor replay — 2026-10-13

The existing Studio structural fusion was the strongest treatment where a complete graph was available. Some individual native patterns improved retrieval, but the new distinctions did not beat that existing recipe. Across the full eligible population, grouped selection chose the Qwen-equivalent identity arm in every fold. Graph availability, not just ranking quality, is a substantial limitation.

No embedding or Jev acquisition ran. Production extraction, admission, joins, ranking, and metrics executed as native Flix Bazel actions on BuildBuddy. Launchers compiling were not counted as completed experiments.

## Frozen treatments

[PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) froze the grammar, metric, folds, support, fusion, empty-pattern behavior, and budgets before outcomes. [PROTOCOL-AMENDMENT-RBE80.md](PROTOCOL-AMENDMENT-RBE80.md) changes execution concurrency only. Sixty-four native patterns were selected deterministically from 113 candidates; 49 cap-excluded candidates remain inventoried. The 595 configurations include all controls. There was no adaptive refinement or combination search.

Rules use typed File/Symbol participants and a validated single world. They are native first-class Datalog fragments, not interpreted descriptors. The named catalogue includes chains, shared dependencies/consumers, triangles, cycles, a distinct-intermediate diamond, owned calls, specific shared callers/callees, same-caller versus same-file evidence, and mixed import/call agreement. The six-atom, seven-variable same-file contrast is a disclosed exception to the suggested six-variable seed bound. [PATTERNS.md](PATTERNS.md) gives all identities, readable rules, native syntax, requirements, and witnesses.

Anchors are Qwen's first three or five files. Support either counts distinct anchors or computes

`support(candidate) = sum(1 / distinct_supported_candidates(anchor))`

over supporting anchors. Repeated proof paths do not add votes. Agreement variants require at least two distinct anchor files. Fusion uses current Studio reciprocal-rank fusion, constant 60 and deterministic path ties. Fixed-pool arms reorder Qwen32 and retain the original tail. Expansion arms use the frozen fused order to choose at most 32 files and retain the remaining Qwen order. A completed empty pattern returns the baseline unchanged; an execution failure is not an empty successful pattern.

## What helped, and what did not

The following are repository-macro percentages over the **47 completed eligible tasks / 14 groups**. These configurations were frozen, but highlighting the best individual configuration is descriptive outcome selection, not a deployable selector.

| Treatment | Recall@10 | MRR@10 | FileAcc@10 | Pool Recall@32 | FileAcc@32 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen | 62.424 | 65.238 | 43.452 | 81.387 | 70.238 |
| Current Studio fusion | **73.810** | 62.689 | **54.762** | 80.330 | 63.095 |
| ImportCallAgreement, five anchors, votes, expansion | 67.781 | 50.357 | 48.810 | **86.149** | **75.595** |
| DirectImport, five anchors, votes, fixed pool | 67.767 | 64.404 | 47.024 | 81.387 | 70.238 |
| DirectImport, five anchors, votes, expansion | 62.425 | 57.971 | 45.238 | 85.073 | 73.214 |
| SharedCallee, five anchors, normalized, fixed pool | 66.920 | 49.328 | 45.833 | 81.387 | 70.238 |

ImportCallAgreement improved Recall@10 on five tasks and harmed one. Its 0.014 percentage-point advantage over direct-import fixed-pool reranking is not persuasive evidence that the extra constraint is generally better. Against equally budgeted direct-import expansion it improved both final Recall@10 and pool coverage on this subset. It added five gold and 62 non-gold files outside Qwen32, displacing no gold. Those are real candidate recoveries within the same 32-file budget.

Current Studio fusion improved Recall@10 on 12 tasks and harmed none in this subset. Gains occurred in eight of 14 groups; six were unchanged. Leaving out one group changes its mean improvement from 9.70 to 12.26 percentage points. It added eight gold and displaced six while adding 488 non-gold files to pools. Pool macro Recall@32 nevertheless decreased: heterogeneous task denominators and repository weighting mean added/displaced file totals do not determine a macro score. Its MRR also decreased. The score is not an across-the-board win.

SharedCallee and SharedConsumer show complementary gains and losses. SameCaller and SameFileDistinctCallers produced different witnesses and outcomes: their best descriptive Recall@10 values were 63.317% and 62.180%, respectively. This validates the distinction's operational meaning without establishing a general localization benefit. SharedDependency performed poorly even in its best variant: 46.171% Recall@10, four improved tasks and 14 harmed. Some cycle/agreement arms had no support and correctly returned Qwen unchanged. Full per-pattern and per-configuration results retain all of these null and negative observations.

## Selection and uncertainty

The primary 85-task / 22-group population gives Qwen and Identity 63.8909% repository-macro Recall@10, compared with 42.9928% for StudioCurrent when its unavailable executions remain misses. Every leave-one-repository-out training selection chose Identity, including the prescribed MRR, complexity, and lexical tie-breaks. All revisions of a repository stay together. Selections were sealed before held-out outcome tables were inspected.

The primary out-of-fold procedure therefore reproduces Qwen: macro Recall@10 `306983/480480`; task-micro Recall@10 `198127/309400`. No pristine holdout is claimed. Individual configuration tables are descriptive; no uncorrected best-of-hundreds significance claim is made. The completed subset contains uneven numbers of tasks per group, which are explicit in the tables.

Hindsight task-wise and repository-wise oracle selection reach 75.7801% and 74.0755% macro Recall@10 across all eligible tasks. These are headroom diagnostics with access to labels, not selectors. Improvements are partly complementary and concentrated; the full-cohort held-out selector erases them because availability and reliability matter.

## Execution and cost

The first full deterministic cold scientific pass took 1,134.297 seconds (18.9 minutes). It attempted extraction on every world, with acquired vectors and prepared toolchains retained. The witness-order repair took a separate 69.702 seconds. Identical warm reconstruction took 0.439 seconds; a new empty client took 62.032 seconds and received all 707 scientific payloads from remote cache. Independent recomputation took 564.443 seconds, including six whole-world extractions and all 88 Qwen rankings plus 86 pattern/fusion runs. These are different workload accounts, not a claimed whole-system speedup ratio.

The campaign used 80 Bazel jobs on authorized arm64 RBE, with local CPU restricted to orchestration. An initially hidden host-sized input-upload semaphore was diagnosed and disabled with the installed Bazel flag `--noexperimental_throttle_remote_action_building`. World-level extraction remains a coarse boundary: one slow world can dominate a tail even with 80 available cores. The four worlds exceeding the frozen ten-minute limit were pruned from retries by execution cost, before outcome selection.

Join work is shared across all 64 patterns per task; ranking work is shared across 595 configurations. Per-pattern marginal execution cost was not separately measured. [The cost figure](figures/quality-shared-cost.png) explicitly uses the measured shared action cost. Java timing encloses the consumed serialized Flix result; compilation is recorded separately. Cache hits are replay observations, not new compute samples. There is no warmed local-kernel or whole-agent speed claim.

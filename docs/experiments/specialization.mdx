The specialization follow-up asked whether structural response differences repeat across issues and justify a repository-specific policy. It reused 85 eligible independent issue groups from the previously exposed panel, with physically separated calibration and held labels.

A shrinkage selector used fixed strength two and repository-equal pooling. Other procedures used local effects, pooled defaults, or imports only. Profiles were sealed before application; labels first entered evaluation.

| Operational procedure | Repository-macro Recall@10 | Fallback tasks |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Qwen | 63.8909% | 0/85 |
| Fixed Studio with Qwen fallback | 70.0004% | 38/85 |
| Learned pooled policy | 66.9431% | 38/85 |
| Primary shrinkage | 67.7110% | 38/85 |
| Local policy | 65.9555% | 3/85 |
| Imports-only selector | 63.7653% | 38/85 |

Primary specialization improved on Qwen and the learned pooled default, but lost 2.2894 percentage points to fixed Studio. It helped React while errors elsewhere erased that advantage. Own-versus-transferred profile advantage remained unresolved under repository-omission sensitivities.

Only 47/85 tasks completed structural execution. Eight repository groups were entirely fallback-only and therefore structurally unmeasured. Explicit fallback recovers operational utility; it does not turn unavailable graphs into measurements.

Forty-two informative calibration/held episodes had median tie-aware response correlation 0.253; 43 constant profiles were uninformative. Within-repository issue variance exceeded between-repository variance. This limits a unique repository-fingerprint interpretation.
